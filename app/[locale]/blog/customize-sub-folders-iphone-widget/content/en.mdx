# How to Give Each Sub-Folder Its Own Look in an iPhone Widget

**Quick answer:** Apple's Home Screen folders all look the same. In a [WidgetLoft](/widgetloft) widget, each sub-folder can now have its own style: open the sub-folder, tap **Appearance**, and set its own icon size, app names, spacing and colors. You can also give the folder tile a photo or symbol, restyle the back tile, and choose how folders open.

A "Games" folder with big, colorful icons and a "Work" folder with small, quiet ones is easier to read at a glance. Now one widget can hold both.

---

## Why give sub-folders their own look

Sub-folders let one widget hold far more apps than it has slots: each folder holds up to 100 apps and 20 sub-folders, and nesting has no depth limit. When you tap a folder on the widget, it opens in place.

The catch used to be that every level looked the same. A folder with 6 apps had the same tiny icons as one with 40. With a look per sub-folder, a short folder can use big icons, and a long one can use small ones to fit everything on one screen.

## Step-by-step: style a sub-folder

1. **Open the sub-folder.** In WidgetLoft, open the group, then the sub-folder you want to change.
2. **Tap Appearance.** Set this sub-folder's own **icon size**, **app names** (on or off), **spacing** and **colors**. The rest of the group stays as it is.
3. **Pick the folder tile.** Choose what the folder looks like from outside: a 2×2 or 3×3 mini-grid of its apps, your own photo or an SF Symbol.
4. **Style the back tile.** Pick its symbol, color, background, opacity and size, and drag it to any slot. Under **Use This Look For**, apply it to this folder, this group or every group, and choose whether new sub-folders start with it.
5. **Choose the animation.** In WidgetLoft **Settings**, set **Sub-folder transition** to Slide, Fade, Zoom or Instant. Instant is the fastest.

## What you can change, and where

| Setting | Where | Applies to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Icon size, names, spacing, colors | Sub-folder → Appearance | That sub-folder |
| Folder tile (mini-grid, photo, symbol) | Sub-folder settings | How it looks from outside |
| Back tile | Sub-folder → back tile | Folder, group or every group |
| Opening animation | Settings → Sub-folder transition | All widgets |

Need to reorganize later? You can move a sub-folder to another group, or copy it, and everything inside goes with it. New to nesting? Start with [how to put folders inside folders on iPhone](/blog/folders-inside-folders-iphone).

## Dots on folders

On iOS 27, a folder shows a notification dot when an app inside it has one, so you do not miss a message hidden two levels deep. By default it shows how many notifications arrived; for a plain dot, turn off **Settings → Notification Dots → Show the number**. For the full setup, read [notification badges on widgets](/blog/notification-badges-on-widgets-iphone).

## Common questions

**Can folders inside folders look different on iPhone?**
Not with Apple's folders. In a WidgetLoft widget each sub-folder can have its own icon size, app names, spacing and colors.

**How many sub-folders can I make?**
Each folder holds up to 100 apps and 20 sub-folders, with no depth limit.

**Do sub-folders show notification dots?**
Yes, on iOS 27. A folder shows a dot when an app inside it has one, with the number of notifications by default.
