# iPadOS 27 Windowing: Build a Home Screen That Keeps Up

**Quick answer:** iPadOS 27 keeps the desktop-style windowing of iPadOS 26 and makes opening, resizing and switching windows faster. That changes what a good Home Screen looks like: you now open several apps in a row rather than one at a time, so the page should put a lot of apps one tap away. A launcher widget does that better than folders.

---

## What changed, briefly

iPadOS 26 turned the iPad into a windowed machine. iPadOS 27 keeps going in the same direction: windows respond faster, iPhone apps running on an iPad can be resized, and the Home Screen itself gains undo and redo while you are rearranging icons.

Two smaller changes matter for layout. The Lock Screen clock can be repositioned, so your wallpaper is no longer half covered. And Liquid Glass now has a slider in Settings, so you decide how transparent the interface looks.

## Why windows change your Home Screen

With one app at a time, the Home Screen is a place you pass through. With windows, it is a staging area. You go back to it to pull in the second, third and fourth app of a task, and you do that many times a day.

That makes two things expensive:

- **Folders.** Two taps each, every time.
- **Search.** Fast for one app, slow as a habit, and it pulls your hands off the layout.

What you want instead is a page where twenty or thirty apps are visible and one tap away.

## The layout

1. **One launcher widget, top of the page.** Add a wide Extra Large widget from [WidgetLoft](/widgetloft) and point it at a group with every app you open during work. Small icons fit up to 72 tiles; there is a [full capacity table in the iPad launcher guide](/blog/ipad-app-launcher-widget).
2. **Group by task, not by type.** A window-based workflow opens apps in clusters: notes plus browser plus mail. Put clusters together in the grid, or give each its own folder tile.
3. **Keep the dock for the two or three apps you always have open.** The dock survives every page, so it is the wrong place for anything occasional.
4. **Leave the bottom half of the page empty.** That is where windows land. A page crowded to the edges fights the thing you are actually doing.

Each tile opens its app as a normal window, because the tile launches the app exactly the way its icon does.

## Sub-folders instead of pages

Swiping between Home Screen pages is slower than it looks, and with windows open you often cannot see the page dots at all. A launcher widget's folder tiles open **in place**: tap a folder tile, the grid switches to its contents, tap the back tile to come up.

That gives you the depth of several pages without leaving the page. Each folder holds up to 100 apps and 20 sub-folders, and you can nest as deep as you like. Turn on **Auto-return to folder** in Settings, Widget, so the widget resets itself to the top level instead of sitting where you left it.

## Two more things worth setting

**Undo while arranging.** Long-press the Home Screen until the icons jiggle and you will see undo and redo. Move things freely; you are no longer committing.

**The Liquid Glass slider.** Settings, Appearance, Liquid Glass. Pull it toward Clear for a lighter interface, toward Tinted if you want text and controls to read harder against a busy wallpaper. It redraws live as you drag.

## Common questions

**Does opening an app from a widget give it a window?**
Yes. The tile launches the app the same way the icon does, so it opens as a window and behaves like any other.

**Do I still need app folders on iPad?**
Less than before. Folder tiles inside a launcher widget cover the same need without leaving the page.

**Can I undo a Home Screen rearrangement on iPadOS 27?**
Yes, undo and redo are there while the icons jiggle.
